Case Study · Behavior Change

Cybersecurity Behavior Change

A public-safe summary of enterprise security and safety enablement designed to move employees from passive awareness toward practical vigilance and reporting behavior.

Organization
Roku
Industry
Media technology / streaming
Company size
3,000+ employees globally
Focus areas
Behavior change, cybersecurity awareness, risk communication

Organizational Context

Security and safety risks required employees to recognize suspicious behavior, social engineering, phishing attempts, visitor risk, and emergency scenarios. The work needed to make risk concrete and actionable for a broad employee population.

Environmental Factors

  • Enterprise-wide security and safety responsibilities.
  • Need for consistent reporting behavior and practical vigilance.
  • Multiple specialist SME domains across security, safety, privacy, and workplace risk.

The Challenge

Passive awareness was not enough. Employees needed to recognize specific risk signals, understand reporting paths, and respond appropriately without overloading them with policy detail.

Key Friction Points

  • Security topics can feel abstract until employees see realistic scenarios.
  • Risk response depends on behavior under uncertainty.
  • SME knowledge had to be translated into clear employee action.

Strategy & Systems Design

The approach was to build behavior-oriented security and safety learning that emphasized recognition, decision-making, and reporting behavior.

Systems Built

  • A 10-module Gatekeeper Security Training suite.
  • Cybersecurity and information security training transformation.
  • Phishing and social engineering learning that emphasized practical inspection and response behavior.

Why This Approach

Security enablement needed to build judgment and vigilance, not just knowledge of policy. Scenario-based learning and clear reporting expectations were better suited to that goal than passive information delivery.

Implementation & Rollout

The work was built with specialist SMEs and rolled out to an enterprise audience. Public details are limited to high-level structure and evidence signals.

Content / Asset Types

  • Security and safety modules.
  • Scenario-based examples and recognition prompts.
  • Communication and launch support.
  • Reporting and response guidance.

Outcomes & Signals

Quantitative Signals

  • 10-module security training suite.
  • 600+ attendee launch webcast.
  • Campaign engagement around 40% open rates.

Qualitative Outcomes

  • Increased threat reporting after the training transformation.
  • Greater employee vigilance, including increased false-positive reporting as a behavioral signal.
  • Clearer employee response expectations across safety and security scenarios.

Specific operational metrics and internal materials are omitted from this public version while preserving the overall scope, approach, and outcomes of the work.

Lessons Learned

Security learning works best when it treats employees as active participants in risk detection. The goal is not just awareness; it is recognition, judgment, and reporting behavior.

Case Study · Leadership Systems

Manager Capability & Leadership Enablement

A public-safe summary of manager enablement architecture designed to turn leadership expectations into observable behaviors, practice loops, and usable playbooks.

Organization
Roku
Industry
Media technology / streaming
Audience
Managers and leadership stakeholders
Focus areas
Manager enablement, behavior change, leadership systems

Organizational Context

Leadership themes needed to become practical manager behaviors that could be practiced, reinforced, and observed in day-to-day work.

Environmental Factors

  • Need for consistent manager behaviors across teams.
  • Broad leadership expectations that required operational clarity.
  • Need to connect leadership development to real work rather than abstract theory.

The Challenge

The challenge was to avoid generic soft-skills training and instead design a system that translated leadership expectations into observable behaviors, micro-skills, and practice routines.

Key Friction Points

  • Leadership concepts can remain too abstract to change behavior.
  • Managers need usable practices they can apply in existing work rhythms.
  • Behavior change requires repetition, reflection, and reinforcement.

Strategy & Systems Design

The approach was to build a manager capability system around behavior clusters, playbooks, micro-skills, practice assignments, anti-patterns, and adoption indicators.

Systems Built

  • Manager playbook architecture.
  • Behavior clusters for topics such as psychological safety, coaching, accountability, friction removal, and stakeholder navigation.
  • Practice-loop model for real-work application and debrief.

Conceptual System View

Leadership concepts and team needs

Why This Approach

Leadership development needed to move beyond conceptual content. The design centered on behaviors managers could practice in real meetings, 1:1s, feedback moments, and team operating rhythms.

Implementation & Rollout

This public version focuses on the overall architecture, behavior model, and systems-design approach while omitting internal playbooks and implementation-specific details.

Content / Asset Types

  • Manager playbooks.
  • Practice assignments.
  • Facilitation structures.
  • Behavior definitions and anti-patterns.
  • Adoption indicators.

Outcomes & Signals

Qualitative Outcomes

  • Translated leadership themes into practical manager behaviors.
  • Created a structure for practice, reflection, and reinforcement.
  • Connected manager capability to work quality, clarity, psychological safety, and accountability.

Specific rollout details, internal materials, and program metrics are intentionally omitted from this public version.

Lessons Learned

Manager enablement works best when leadership expectations become visible behaviors. Playbooks are useful only when paired with practice, feedback, and reinforcement inside normal work rhythms.
